CI is green, tests are passing, and the release is ready. Then someone opens the product on an old tablet, over a slow connection, or following a path that was never anticipated. And there it is: the issue nobody saw coming.
Automation protects against regressions, but it cannot explore every usage context. Doing so requires diverse perspectives: the person who built the feature, the one who designed it, the one who sells it, and the one who uses it every day.

A Bug Bash is not an improvised manual testing session. It is a structured event where the entire team tests and attempts to break the product together—with clear mission charters, mixed pairs, and a streamlined process to log and evaluate findings.
In this talk, we will see how to organize a Bug Bash without causing chaos: picking the right timing, crafting charters that empower non-QA teammates, avoiding vague bug reports, and applying gamification to engage people without devolving into a race for the most trivial bug.

By the end, you'll have a turnkey format ready to take into your next sprint. Not just to find more tickets, but to make quality, risk awareness, and product decisions a true team-wide responsibility.
